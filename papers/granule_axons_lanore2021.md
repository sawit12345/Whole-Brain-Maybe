# Fallback note (publisher paywall; abstract + figure captions captured)

Lanore F, Cayco-Gajic NA, Gurnani H, Coyle D, Silver RA (2021).
"Cerebellar granule cell axons support high-dimensional representations."
Nature Neuroscience 24:1142–1150. DOI: 10.1038/s41593-021-00873-x
URL: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-021-00873-x

## Key points (from fetched article page)
- Motivation: classical Marr/Albus theories require high-dimensional granule
  cell (GrC) representations for pattern separation; recent calcium-imaging
  studies had suggested GrC population activity is LOW-dimensional.
- Method: 3D acousto-optic-lens two-photon microscopy recording from hundreds
  of GrC AXONS (parallel fibers) in vivo during spontaneous behaviors,
  analyzing representations from the viewpoint of downstream Purkinje-cell
  decoders.
- Results: GrC axon population activity is HIGH-dimensional and spatially
  distributed with little fine-scale structure; distinct behavioral states are
  represented along ORTHOGONAL dimensions in activity space.
- Conclusion: cerebellar cortex supports high-dimensional representations and
  segregates behavioral-state-dependent computations into orthogonal
  subspaces (as reported in neocortex), matching pattern-separation theory
  predictions despite very different circuitry.
- Data: https://doi.org/10.5522/04/14482977 ; code:
  https://github.com/SilverLabUCL/ParallelFibres
